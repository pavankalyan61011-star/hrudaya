## Why you were in hospital
You came in with unstable angina. This means chest pain caused by a heart artery that is not getting enough blood. You were admitted on 2026-10-06 and discharged on 2026-10-08.

## What was done
The doctors reached your heart through an artery in your right wrist (radial access). They took an angiogram, which is an X-ray picture of the heart arteries. One drug-eluting stent was placed in the proximal LAD (left anterior descending artery, a main artery on the front of the heart). A stent is a tiny mesh tube that holds the artery open. Balloons were also used to prepare and shape the artery. No complications were recorded.

## What we found
- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Proximal LAD: 90% narrowing. This was the artery that needed treatment. After the stent, the narrowing is 5%.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery): 30% narrowing.
- Mid RCA (right coronary artery): 40% narrowing.

Blood flow (TIMI 3) was normal in all these arteries.
