## Why you were in hospital
You had a heart attack (STEMI). One of the blood vessels supplying your heart was fully blocked. This was an emergency. You came in on 2026-09-19 and went home on 2026-09-23.

## What was done
Your doctor reached your heart through an artery in your right wrist (radial access). A dye test (angiogram) showed the blockages. One drug-eluting stent, a small mesh tube that keeps the vessel open, was placed in the proximal LAD (left anterior descending artery, a main vessel on the front of the heart). Balloons were used to open the vessel and fit the stent.

## What we found
- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Proximal LAD: 100% narrowing, meaning fully blocked. After the stent, 5% narrowing remained and blood flow was normal.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery): 30% narrowing.
- Mid RCA (right coronary artery): 40% narrowing.

## Your heart pump
Your LVEF (the share of blood your heart pumps out with each beat) was 45%.
