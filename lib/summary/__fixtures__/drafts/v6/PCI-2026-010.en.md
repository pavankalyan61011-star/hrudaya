## Why you were in hospital
You had a heart attack (STEMI, a type where an artery is fully blocked). You came in as an emergency on 2026-09-21 and went home on 2026-09-25.

## What was done
Your doctor reached your heart through the artery at your right wrist (radial route). An angiogram, an X-ray test with dye, showed the arteries. One drug-eluting stent, a small mesh tube that releases medicine to keep the artery open, was placed in the proximal RCA (right coronary artery, near its start). Balloons were used to prepare and open the artery.

During the procedure, blood flow slowed after the stent was placed (no-reflow). The team checked and excluded a tear in the artery wall (dissection). Medicine given directly into the artery restored normal blood flow (TIMI III), and the problem resolved.

## What we found
- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid LAD (left anterior descending artery): 40% narrowing.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery): 30% narrowing.
- Proximal RCA: 100% narrowing, fully blocked. After the stent, 5% narrowing remained.

## Your heart pump
Your heart's pumping strength (LVEF) was 45%.
