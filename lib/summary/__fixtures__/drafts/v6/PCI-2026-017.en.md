## Why you were in hospital
You came in with unstable angina. This means chest pain from the heart that comes on suddenly or worsens, because part of the heart muscle is not getting enough blood. It needed urgent attention. You were admitted on 2026-10-06 and discharged on 2026-10-08.

## What was done
Your doctor did an angiogram, which is an X-ray test that shows the arteries of the heart. The tube was passed through an artery in your right wrist (radial access). One drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that releases medicine to keep the artery open) was placed in the proximal LAD (the left anterior descending artery, a main artery on the front of the heart).

## What we found
- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing
- Proximal LAD: 90% narrowing. This was the blocked artery that was treated, and the narrowing after the stent was 5%.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery): 30% narrowing
- Mid RCA (right coronary artery): 40% narrowing

Blood flow in all of these arteries was normal (TIMI flow 3).
