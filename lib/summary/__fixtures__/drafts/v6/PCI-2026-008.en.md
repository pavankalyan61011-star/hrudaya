## Why you were in hospital
You had a heart attack, called ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). This is an emergency in which an artery supplying the heart muscle becomes blocked. You were admitted on 2026-09-17 and discharged on 2026-09-20.

## What was done
Your doctors used an angiogram (an X-ray test with dye to see the heart arteries). The thin tube was passed through the artery at your right wrist (radial access). One drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that releases medicine and keeps the artery open) was placed in the mid RCA (right coronary artery). Balloons were used to open the artery and fit the stent.

## What we found
- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid LAD (left anterior descending artery): 30% narrowing.
- LCX (left circumflex artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid RCA: 100% narrowing, meaning it was fully blocked. After the stent, the narrowing was 5% and blood flow was normal.

## Your heart pump
Your heart's pumping strength (LVEF) was 50%.
