## Why you were in hospital

You came in for a planned procedure because of stable angina (chest pain on effort, caused by narrowed heart arteries). You also have type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, and you have had a stent procedure (PCI) before.

## What was done

Your heart arteries were examined by angiogram (an X-ray scan using dye). The doctor reached your heart through the artery at your right wrist (radial route). The narrowed mid RCA (right coronary artery, the main artery on the right side of the heart) was opened with a scoring balloon, then treated with a paclitaxel-coated balloon (a balloon that releases medicine onto the artery wall). No stent was placed.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid LAD (left anterior descending artery, at the front of the heart): 30% narrowing.
- LCX (left circumflex artery, on the left side): 0% narrowing.
- Mid RCA: 80% narrowing before treatment, about 20% afterwards.

Blood flow in all these arteries was normal (TIMI flow 3).
