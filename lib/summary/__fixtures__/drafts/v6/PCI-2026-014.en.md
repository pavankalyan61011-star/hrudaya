## Why you were in hospital

You came in for a planned procedure because of stable angina. This is chest discomfort that comes from the heart not getting enough blood through narrowed arteries. You also have high blood pressure and high cholesterol.

## What was done

The doctors used the right wrist artery (radial route) to reach your heart. They took an angiogram, which is an X-ray picture of the heart arteries made visible with dye. Two drug-eluting stents were placed, one in the mid LCX (left circumflex artery) and one in the mid RCA (right coronary artery). A stent is a small mesh tube that holds a narrowed artery open and slowly releases medicine to keep it from narrowing again. No complications were recorded.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing, no blockage.
- Mid LAD (left anterior descending artery): 30% narrowing.
- Mid LCX: 85% narrowing. After the stent, about 5% narrowing remains.
- Mid RCA: 90% narrowing. After the stent, about 5% narrowing remains.

Blood flow in all these arteries was normal after the procedure.

## Looking after yourself

- Take your DAPT tablets regularly, every day, as prescribed.
- Keep the wrist puncture site clean and look after it carefully.
- Follow the activity advice given to you by your team.

## When to come back urgently

Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:

- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:

- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
