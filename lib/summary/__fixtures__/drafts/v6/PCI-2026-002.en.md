## Why you were in hospital

You had a heart attack called a non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI). It was urgent, so you were admitted on 2026-09-02. You were discharged on 2026-09-04.

## What was done

The doctors took X-ray pictures of the arteries of your heart (an angiogram). The tube was passed through the artery in your right wrist. One drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that releases medicine and holds the artery open) was placed in the mid LAD (left anterior descending artery, a main artery on the front of the heart).

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid LAD: 95% narrowing. This was the blocked artery that was treated. After the stent, about 5% narrowing remained.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery): 30% narrowing.
- Proximal RCA (right coronary artery): 40% narrowing.

## Looking after yourself

- Please attend a rehabilitation programme. Your team will refer you.
- Keep the wrist puncture site clean and look after it carefully.
- Take your DAPT tablets every day, as prescribed.

## When to come back urgently

Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:

- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:

- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
