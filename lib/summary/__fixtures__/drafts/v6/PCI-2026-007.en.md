## Why you were in hospital

You had a heart attack called NSTEMI (non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction). This happens when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is reduced. You came in on 14 September 2026 and went home on 16 September 2026.

## What was done

You had an urgent angiogram (an X-ray test of the heart's blood vessels). The doctor reached your heart through the artery in your right wrist (radial route). A narrowed artery at the back of the heart, the distal RCA (right coronary artery), was opened. A drug-eluting stent, a small mesh tube that slowly releases medicine, was placed there. Balloons were used to prepare the artery and settle the stent.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Proximal LAD (left anterior descending artery, upper part): 30% narrowing.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery, middle part): 40% narrowing.
- Distal RCA: 90% narrowing. This was the artery that was stented. After the stent, about 5% narrowing remained.

## Looking after yourself

- Please take up the rehabilitation programme you have been referred to.
- Take care to avoid bleeding, as your tablets make bleeding more likely.
- Keep the wrist puncture site clean and look after it as you were shown.

## When to come back urgently

Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:

- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:

- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
