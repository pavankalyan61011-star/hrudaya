## Why you were in hospital

You had a heart attack called a non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI). This needed urgent treatment. You were in hospital from 2026-09-09 to 2026-09-12. Case code: PCI-2026-005.

## What was done

A thin tube was passed through an artery in your right groin (femoral route) to take X-ray pictures of the arteries of your heart (angiogram). One drug-eluting stent, a small mesh tube that releases medicine, was placed in the mid RCA (right coronary artery). Balloons were used to open the artery and to fit the stent.

After the tube was removed, you developed a 3-cm bruise or swelling in the groin (haematoma). It was treated with firm pressure and watching. Your blood count (haemoglobin) stayed stable, and an ultrasound scan showed there was no pseudoaneurysm (a blood-filled pouch in the artery wall). The swelling settled with pressure and needed no procedure.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid LAD (left anterior descending artery): 40% narrowing.
- LCX (left circumflex artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid RCA: 90% narrowing, which slowed blood flow. After the stent, the narrowing was 5% and blood flow was normal.
