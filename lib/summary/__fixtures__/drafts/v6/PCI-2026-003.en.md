## Why you were in hospital

You had a heart attack called a non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI). It needed urgent treatment. You were admitted on 2026-09-05 and discharged on 2026-09-07.

## What was done

A thin tube was passed through an artery in your right wrist (radial access) to take X-ray pictures of the arteries of your heart (angiogram). One drug-eluting stent, a small mesh tube that releases medicine, was placed in the proximal LCX (the first part of the left circumflex artery). It was opened with balloons to widen the artery.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Proximal LAD (left anterior descending artery): 75% narrowing.
- Proximal LCX: 90% narrowing. This is the artery that was stented. After the stent, 5% narrowing remained and blood flow was normal.
- Mid RCA (right coronary artery): 30% narrowing.
