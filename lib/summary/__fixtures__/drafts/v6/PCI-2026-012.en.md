## Why you were in hospital
You had a heart attack called a STEMI (ST-elevation myocardial infarction). It was an emergency. You were admitted on 2026-09-26 and discharged on 2026-09-30. Your risk factors are high blood pressure and high cholesterol.

## What was done
Your doctor reached your heart arteries through a blood vessel in the right groin (femoral artery). An angiogram (X-ray pictures of the heart arteries) was done. One drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that releases medicine) was placed in the mid LAD (left anterior descending artery). Balloons were used to open the artery and to expand the stent.

## What we found
- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid LAD: 100% narrowing, a complete blockage. This was the artery that was stented. Narrowing after the stent was 5%.
- Proximal LCX (left circumflex artery): 75% narrowing.
- Mid RCA (right coronary artery): 30% narrowing.

## Your heart pump
Your LVEF (the share of blood your heart pumps out with each beat) is 45%.

## Looking after yourself
- Plan follow-up to arrange treatment for the narrowing in your LCX.
- Take care of your groin wound.
- Take part in a heart rehabilitation programme.
- Take your DAPT tablets regularly, as prescribed.

## When to come back urgently
Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:
- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the groin puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The foot on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:
- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
