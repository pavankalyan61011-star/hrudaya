## Why you were in hospital
You had a heart attack called NSTEMI (non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction). It happened because blood flow in your heart arteries was reduced. You came in urgently on 2026-09-07 and went home on 2026-09-10.

## What was done
Your doctor reached your heart through the artery in your right wrist (radial access). Dye was used to take pictures of your heart arteries (angiogram). Two drug-eluting stents were placed. A stent is a small mesh tube that holds an artery open and slowly releases medicine. One stent went into the proximal RCA (right coronary artery, first part) and one into the mid LAD (left anterior descending artery, middle part).

## What we found
- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Mid LAD: 85% narrowing.
- LCX (left circumflex artery): 10% narrowing.
- Proximal RCA: 95% narrowing. This was the tightest area.

After stenting, the narrowing in both treated arteries was 5%.

## Looking after yourself
- Stop smoking.
- Take part in a heart rehabilitation programme.
- Take your DAPT tablets regularly, as prescribed.

## When to come back urgently
Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:
- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:
- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
