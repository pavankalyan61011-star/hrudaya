## Why you were in hospital

You had a heart attack of the type called NSTEMI (non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction). This means a heart artery was badly narrowed and part of your heart muscle was not getting enough blood. You came in urgently on 2026-09-11 and went home on 2026-09-13.

## What was done

The doctor reached your heart arteries through a small cut in the left wrist (radial artery). A dye was used to take X-ray pictures of the arteries (angiogram). The badly narrowed artery, the mid LCX (middle part of the left circumflex artery), was opened with balloons. One drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that releases medicine) was placed there to keep it open.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing. Blood flow was normal.
- LAD (left anterior descending artery): 10% narrowing. Blood flow was normal.
- Proximal RCA (first part of the right coronary artery): 20% narrowing. Blood flow was normal.
- Mid LCX: 95% narrowing, with slowed blood flow. This was the artery causing your heart attack. After the stent, the narrowing was 5% and blood flow was normal.

## Looking after yourself

- Keep up your diabetes review.
- Take up the rehabilitation programme you have been referred to.
- Take your DAPT tablets regularly, exactly as prescribed.

## When to come back urgently

Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:

- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:

- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
