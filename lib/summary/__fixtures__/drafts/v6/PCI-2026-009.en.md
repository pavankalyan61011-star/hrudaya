## Why you were in hospital

You had a heart attack. This type is called a STEMI (ST-elevation myocardial infarction). It was an emergency. You were admitted on 2026-09-19 and discharged on 2026-09-23. Case code: PCI-2026-009.

## What was done

The doctors reached your heart through an artery in your right wrist (radial access). They took X-ray pictures of the heart arteries (angiogram). One artery, the proximal LAD (left anterior descending artery, the front part of the main artery on the left), was fully blocked. One drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that holds the artery open and releases medicine) was placed there.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing.
- Proximal LAD: 100% narrowing, fully blocked. After the stent, 5% narrowing remained and blood flow was normal.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery): 30% narrowing.
- Mid RCA (right coronary artery): 40% narrowing.

## When to come back urgently

Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:

- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:

- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
