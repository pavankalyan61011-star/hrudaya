## Why you were in hospital
You came for a planned check of your heart arteries because of stable angina (chest discomfort that comes on with effort). You also have type 2 diabetes and high cholesterol, and you used to smoke.

## What was done
A thin tube was passed through an artery in your right wrist (radial access, 5 Fr sheath). Dye was used to take X-ray pictures of the arteries of your heart (coronary angiogram). No stent or balloon treatment was done.

## What we found
- Left main artery (LMCA): 0% narrowing.
- Front artery (proximal LAD, left anterior descending): 30% narrowing.
- Side artery (mid LCX, left circumflex): 20% narrowing.
- Right artery (mid RCA, right coronary artery): 40% narrowing.

Blood flow (TIMI flow 3) was normal in all of these arteries.

## Looking after yourself
- Keep working on your risk factors, such as sugar, cholesterol and smoking.
- Tell your doctor if your chest symptoms come back or change.
- Take care of your wrist puncture site.
- Continue your usual diabetes treatment.

## When to come back urgently
Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:
- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on aspirin)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:
- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
