## Why you were in hospital

You came in for a planned procedure because of stable angina. This is chest discomfort that comes on with effort because part of the heart muscle is not getting enough blood.

## What was done

The doctor passed a thin tube through an artery in your left wrist (radial access) and took X-ray pictures of the arteries of your heart (coronary angiogram). One artery was found to be narrowed. A drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that holds the artery open and slowly releases medicine) was placed in the proximal right coronary artery (proximal RCA). Balloons were used to prepare the artery and to open the stent fully.

## What we found

- Left main coronary artery (LMCA): 0% narrowing
- Left anterior descending artery (LAD): 0% narrowing
- Left circumflex artery (LCX): 10% narrowing
- Proximal RCA: 85% narrowing before treatment, about 5% narrowing after the stent

Blood flow in all these arteries was normal.

## Looking after yourself

- Follow the activity advice given to you and increase your activity gradually.
- Keep the wrist puncture site clean and look after it as you were shown.
- Take your DAPT medicines every day without missing doses.

## When to come back urgently

Go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 108 immediately if you have:

- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back
- Severe breathlessness
- Fainting or collapse
- Bleeding from the wrist puncture site that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- A rapidly growing swelling at the puncture site
- The hand on that side becoming cold, pale, numb or very painful
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood (you are on blood-thinning tablets)

Contact the cardiology team within a day for:

- Increasing pain, redness, warmth or discharge at the puncture site
- Fever
