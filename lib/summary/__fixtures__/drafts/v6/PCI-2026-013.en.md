## Why you were in hospital

You came to hospital for a planned procedure because of stable angina (chest pain from a narrowed heart artery). You also have high blood pressure and high cholesterol. You were admitted on 2026-09-28 and discharged on 2026-09-29.

## What was done

Your doctor reached your heart through the artery at your right wrist (radial access). A coronary angiogram (an X-ray test that shows the heart arteries) was done. One drug-eluting stent (a small mesh tube that releases medicine) was placed in the proximal LAD (left anterior descending artery, a main artery at the front of the heart). It was an Everolimus DES, 3.5 × 20 mm. Balloons were used to prepare and open the artery.

## What we found

- LMCA (left main coronary artery): 0% narrowing
- Proximal LAD: 85% narrowing. After the stent, about 5% narrowing remained.
- Mid LCX (left circumflex artery): 20% narrowing
- RCA (right coronary artery): 0% narrowing

Blood flow in all these arteries was normal (TIMI flow 3).
